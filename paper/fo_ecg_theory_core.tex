\begin{abstract}
We establish the theory gates that precede real-data validation.  The first
result proves that two exact complex rate responses are necessary and
sufficient to recover the order of a scalar fractional ECG harmonic surrogate
with an unknown common complex coefficient and positive real damping.  We then
give an exact multi-rate reference-pair consistency test, prove that
unrestricted rate-specific complex morphology destroys identifiability and
that, under the stated two-rate known-damping interior conditions, any
positive independent complex-disk morphology drift prevents singleton order
recovery, and give a quadratic upper bound on Gram-margin collapse near
real-separable response arrays.
We then give a joint forward-disk feasible-set theorem, a gauge-reduced analytic
nuisance-selector tube with an exact spectral margin and second-order path
enclosure, replayable finite-record component bounds, a frozen-witness
R2-to-T1 transfer theorem, and an exact output-set separation threshold for
worst-case rate-window design.  The complete exact-sample chain is implemented
for a frozen synthetic functional, but the required acquisition and
model-error primitives are not calibrated on the real records.  The real-data
result therefore remains not certifiable.  These statements concern a
declared surface-ECG surrogate, not internal cardiac states.
\end{abstract}

\section{Model, units, and protocol}

Fix one reference time \(\tau_\star>0\) before identification, put
\(\xi=t/\tau_\star\), and use the dimensionless model
\begin{equation}
 {}^C D_\xi^\alpha z(\xi)
 =-\lambda z(\xi)+\sum_{m\in\mathbb Z}q_m e^{im\theta(\xi)}.
 \label{eq:time-model}
\end{equation}
Thus \(\lambda\) is dimensionless and \(q_m\) has the same measurement unit as
\(z\), independently of the candidate order.  A finite-lower-terminal Caputo
system is not assumed to possess an exact periodic orbit
\cite{tavazoei2009}.  The frequency response below is the infinite-history
(Weyl) harmonic branch, equivalently the asymptotic periodic component of the
finite-terminal Caputo solution \cite{duan2022}; all initial-condition,
prehistory, and finite-dwell terms are retained later as R2 residuals.  With
dimensionless frequency \(\nu_{r,m}=m\tau_\star\Omega_r\), where \(\Omega_r\)
is the base angular heart rate in rad/s, one selected positive harmonic has
response
\begin{equation}
 Z_r=\frac{q}{\lambda+(i\nu_r)^\alpha},\qquad
 (i\nu)^\alpha=\nu^\alpha e^{i\pi\alpha/2},
 \label{eq:model}
\end{equation}
where
\[
 \alpha\in I=[\alpha_-,1],\quad 0<\alpha_-<1,\qquad
 \lambda\in\Lambda=[\lambda_-,\lambda_+]\subset\R_{>0},\qquad
 q\in\C\setminus\{0\}.
\]
The coefficient \(q\) is effective in measurement units.  A common nonzero
complex lead gain is absorbed into \(q\).  For a real ECG, the corresponding
negative harmonic is its conjugate and supplies no independent equation.
Throughout, \(\Kset\subset\mathbb Z\) denotes the full estimator basis, which
may contain DC and conjugate negative harmonics, whereas
\(\Mset\subset\mathbb N_{>0}\) contains only the positive target harmonics
passed to R1.  Thus \(\Mset\subseteq\Kset\); DC is an estimator nuisance and a
negative coefficient is a conjugacy check, never an input to a selector that
uses \(\log\nu_{r,m}\).

\begin{assumption}[Information boundary]
\label{ass:protocol}
Rates are known, positive, and distinct.  Complex responses share one R-peak
phase anchor, one causal preprocessing chain, the principal fractional branch,
and common \(q,\lambda,\alpha\).  There is no uncalibrated rate-specific gain,
phase, or delay.  Restitution bounds, when used, are calibrated independently
of the identification record.
\end{assumption}

\section{R0: minimal structural identifiability}

\begin{proposition}[One rate is insufficient]
For one response \(Z_1\), the order is not structurally identifiable.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For every \(\beta\in I\) and \(\ell\in\Lambda\), the nonzero choice
\[
 q_{\beta,\ell}=Z_1[\ell+(i\nu_1)^\beta]
\]
reproduces the same observation.
\end{proof}

For any ordered pair of nonzero responses, put
\[
 W_j=Z_j^{-1},\qquad D=W_2-W_1,
\]
and, whenever \(D\ne0\), define \(C=W_1/D\).

\begin{lemma}[Complete common-gauge invariant]
\label{lem:gauge}
For an ordered nonzero response pair with \(D\ne0\), the quotient \(C\) is
invariant under \((Z_1,Z_2)\mapsto(gZ_1,gZ_2)\), \(g\in\C\setminus\{0\}\).
It is complete: two such ordered pairs have the same \(C\) if and only if one
is obtained from the other by one common nonzero complex gain.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The inverse responses obey \(W_1=CD\) and \(W_2=(C+1)D\).  If a second pair
has difference \(D'\), then \(W'_j=(D'/D)W_j\) and hence
\(Z'_j=(D/D')Z_j\).  The converse follows directly from cancellation in
\(C=W_1/(W_2-W_1)\).
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Quotient-space geometry]
\label{thm:quotient}
For fixed \(\varrho>1\), on the enlarged structural domain before the compact
priors \(I\times\Lambda\) are imposed, the map
\[
 \Phi:(\alpha,\kappa)\longmapsto
 \frac{1+\kappa e^{-i\pi\alpha/2}}{\varrho^\alpha-1}
\]
is one-to-one on \((0,1]\times\R_{>0}\), and its range is
\[
 \mathcal Q_\varrho=
 \left\{u+iv:u\ge\frac{1}{\varrho-1},\ v<0\right\}.
\]
It is a smooth diffeomorphism from
\((0,1)\times\R_{>0}\) onto
\(\{u+iv:u>1/(\varrho-1),v<0\}\), and extends one-to-one to
\(\alpha=1\).  In the interior its real Jacobian is
\begin{equation}
 \det\frac{\partial(\operatorname{Re}C,\operatorname{Im}C)}
          {\partial(\alpha,\kappa)}
 =\frac{\varrho^\alpha\log\varrho\sin(\pi\alpha/2)}
        {(\varrho^\alpha-1)^3}
  +\frac{\kappa\pi}{2(\varrho^\alpha-1)^2}>0.
 \label{eq:jacobian}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Every image point has negative imaginary part; for \(\alpha<1\), its real part is strictly larger than
\(1/(\varrho-1)\), whereas \(\alpha=1\) gives the boundary line.  Conversely,
for \(v<0\), define
\[
 H_{u+iv}(\beta)=(\varrho^\beta-1)
 [u+v\cot(\pi\beta/2)].
\]
The continuous equation \(H_{u+iv}(\beta)=1\) has a root in
\((0,1]\) whenever \((\varrho-1)u\ge1\): its limit at zero is negative and
its value at one is at least one.  The recovered
\[
 \kappa=-\frac{(\varrho^\alpha-1)v}{\sin(\pi\alpha/2)}
\]
is positive.  If \(\alpha\) is one root and \(A_\gamma=\pi\gamma/2\), then
direct substitution gives, for any candidate \(\beta\),
\[
 (\varrho^\alpha-1)[H_{u+iv}(\beta)-1]
 =\varrho^\beta-\varrho^\alpha
 +\kappa(\varrho^\beta-1)
 \frac{\sin(A_\beta-A_\alpha)}{\sin A_\beta}.
\]
Both terms have the sign of \(\beta-\alpha\), so the root and its positive
\(\kappa\) are unique.  Lemma~\ref{lem:gauge} shows that this is the complete
common-gauge quotient.
Direct differentiation gives \eqref{eq:jacobian}; the inverse function
theorem and the established bijection give the interior diffeomorphism.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Global two-rate recovery]
\label{thm:two-rate}
Let \(0<\nu_1<\nu_2\), and suppose two exact complex responses satisfy
\eqref{eq:model}.  Define
\[
 W_r=Z_r^{-1},\quad D=W_2-W_1,\quad
 C=\frac{W_1}{D},\quad \varrho=\frac{\nu_2}{\nu_1}>1.
\]
Then \(D\ne0\), \(\operatorname{Im}C<0\), and the true order is the unique
\(\beta\in(0,1]\) satisfying
\begin{equation}
 H_C(\beta)=1,\qquad
 H_C(\beta)=(\varrho^\beta-1)
 \left[\operatorname{Re}C+
 \operatorname{Im}C\cot\left(\frac{\pi\beta}{2}\right)\right].
 \label{eq:root}
\end{equation}
After recovering \(\alpha\),
\begin{align}
 q&=\frac{e^{i\pi\alpha/2}(\nu_2^\alpha-\nu_1^\alpha)}{D},
 \label{eq:q}\\
 \lambda&=\nu_1^\alpha e^{i\pi\alpha/2}
 [C(\varrho^\alpha-1)-1]\in\R_{>0}.
 \label{eq:lambda}
\end{align}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Inverse-response subtraction gives
\[
 D=\frac{e^{i\pi\alpha/2}(\nu_2^\alpha-\nu_1^\alpha)}{q}\ne0.
\]
With \(\kappa=\lambda/\nu_1^\alpha>0\) and \(A=\pi\alpha/2\),
\begin{equation}
 C=\frac{1+\kappa e^{-iA}}{\varrho^\alpha-1},
 \label{eq:C}
\end{equation}
so \(\operatorname{Im}C<0\).  A candidate \(\beta\) first fixes \(q_\beta\)
through the difference equation.  Substitution into the first response gives
\[
 \lambda_\beta=\nu_1^\beta e^{i\pi\beta/2}
 [C(\varrho^\beta-1)-1].
\]
Writing \(C=u+iv\), \(t=\varrho^\beta-1\), and \(B=\pi\beta/2\),
\[
 \operatorname{Im}\lambda_\beta=0
 \iff (tu-1)\sin B+tv\cos B=0
 \iff H_C(\beta)=1.
\]
Substitute \eqref{eq:C} and use
\[
 \cos A-\sin A\cot B=\frac{\sin(B-A)}{\sin B}.
\]
The candidate equation becomes
\begin{equation}
 \varrho^\beta-\varrho^\alpha+
 \kappa(\varrho^\beta-1)\frac{\sin(B-A)}{\sin B}=0.
 \label{eq:sign}
\end{equation}
Both terms are positive when \(\beta>\alpha\) and negative when
\(\beta<\alpha\); hence \(\beta=\alpha\) is the unique solution.  At a root,
\[
 \operatorname{Re}\lambda_\beta
 =-\nu_1^\beta\frac{t\,\operatorname{Im}C}{\sin B}>0,
\]
and \(D\ne0\) ensures \(q_\beta\ne0\).
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Strict minimum and quotient invariance]
Under Assumption~\ref{ass:protocol}, two distinct rates are the strict
structural minimum.  Under \(Z_r\mapsto gZ_r\),
\(g\in\C\setminus\{0\}\), \(C,\alpha,\lambda\) are invariant, while
\eqref{eq:q} recovers \(gq_{\rm physical}\).
Two exact responses can already exclude the declared model when their
quotient lies outside the physical image or the compact priors.  A third rate
is the minimum that adds a redundant cross-rate consistency equation after an
admissible reference pair has been inverted.
\end{corollary}

\begin{theorem}[Exact reference-pair consistency]
\label{thm:reference-pair-consistency}
Consider the zero-drift single-harmonic model at
\(0<\nu_1<\cdots<\nu_R\), \(R\ge2\), with exact nonzero responses.  Put
\[
 W_r=Z_r^{-1},\qquad
 C_{1r}=\frac{W_1}{W_r-W_1},\qquad
 \varrho_r=\frac{\nu_r}{\nu_1}.
\]
Assume each \(W_r\ne W_1\) and
\(C_{1r}\in\mathcal Q_{\varrho_r}\).  Let
\((\alpha_r,\kappa_r)=\Phi_{\varrho_r}^{-1}(C_{1r})\) be the unique inverse
from Theorem~\ref{thm:quotient}.  On the enlarged structural domain
\((0,1]\times\R_{>0}\times(\C\setminus\{0\})\), all responses arise from one
common \((\alpha,\lambda,q)\) if and only if
\[
 (\alpha_r,\kappa_r)=(\alpha_\star,\kappa_\star)
 \quad\text{for every }r=2,\ldots,R.
\]
Consistency with the declared compact model additionally holds if and only if
\(\alpha_\star\in I\) and
\(\kappa_\star\nu_1^{\alpha_\star}\in\Lambda\).  In either structural case
\[
 \lambda=\kappa_\star\nu_1^{\alpha_\star},\qquad
 q=\frac{\lambda+(i\nu_1)^{\alpha_\star}}{W_1}.
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Necessity follows by substituting the common model into every reference pair.
For sufficiency, the inverse quotient identity gives
\[
 \frac{W_1}{C_{1r}}
 =\frac{(i\nu_r)^{\alpha_\star}-(i\nu_1)^{\alpha_\star}}q.
\]
Since the left side is \(W_r-W_1\), adding
\(W_1=[\lambda+(i\nu_1)^{\alpha_\star}]/q\) proves the model equation for
every rate.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Geometric three-rate cross-ratio]
\label{cor:geometric-three-rate}
In Theorem~\ref{thm:reference-pair-consistency}, take
\(\nu_2=\rho\nu_1\) and \(\nu_3=\rho^2\nu_1\), \(\rho>1\), and define
\[
 Q=\frac{W_3-W_1}{W_2-W_1}.
\]
Every response triple in the declared zero-drift model satisfies the
damping- and morphology-free identity
\[
 Q=1+\rho^\alpha\in\R,\qquad
 \alpha=\frac{\log(Q-1)}{\log\rho}.
\]
Conversely, an admissible reference pair \((1,2)\) with inverse
\((\alpha_\star,\kappa_\star)\) extends to the third response if and only if
\[
 Q=1+\rho^{\alpha_\star}.
\]
Together with
\(\alpha_\star\in I\) and
\(\kappa_\star\nu_1^{\alpha_\star}\in\Lambda\), this is an exact
three-rate model-consistency test.  Thus the third rate is a genuine redundant
equation, not another fitted nuisance parameter.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Under the model,
\[
 W_r-W_1=\frac{(i\nu_r)^\alpha-(i\nu_1)^\alpha}{q}.
\]
The common factors \(q^{-1}(i\nu_1)^\alpha\) cancel, and
\[
 Q=\frac{\rho^{2\alpha}-1}{\rho^\alpha-1}=1+\rho^\alpha.
\]
The logarithmic inverse is unique because \(\rho>1\).  Conversely, the
reference pair fixes \(\alpha_\star,\lambda,q\) by
Theorem~\ref{thm:reference-pair-consistency}; the displayed cross-ratio
identity is exactly the remaining equation \(W_3-W_1\).
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Exact multi-harmonic consistency]
For positive target harmonics in the zero-drift model, apply
Theorem~\ref{thm:reference-pair-consistency} within each harmonic.  A common
\(\alpha_\star,\lambda_\star\) and harmonic-specific \(q_m\) exist if and only
if the reference-pair inverses satisfy
\[
 \alpha_{r,m}=\alpha_\star,\qquad
 \kappa_{r,m}=\kappa_m
 \quad\text{for every }r,m,
\]
and
\[
 \kappa_m\nu_{1,m}^{\alpha_\star}=\lambda_\star
 \quad\text{is independent of }m.
\]
This test does not apply unchanged to the later rate-specific damping-drift
model.  With response disks, pairwise centre inversions are not a certificate;
the complete joint set must be used.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proposition}[Rate-specific complex nuisance destroys identifiability]
\label{prop:rate-specific-no-go}
Let all \(Z_r\ne0\), and remove every annulus or cross-rate constraint on
nonzero \(q_r\in\C\).  In
\[
 Z_r=\frac{q_r}{\lambda+(i\nu_r)^\alpha},
\]
every candidate \((\beta,\ell)\in I\times\R_{>0}\) fits every finite number
of rates by choosing
\[
 q_r=Z_r[\ell+(i\nu_r)^\beta].
\]
The same conclusion holds when an arbitrary unknown nonzero complex gain is
allowed at every rate.  Thus per-window complex normalization removes all
order information carried by the common gauge.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The displayed construction is nonzero and reproduces each response exactly.
An unrestricted rate-specific gain is algebraically equivalent to such an
unrestricted numerator.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Quotient imaginary-axis separation under compact priors]
\label{cor:quotient-imaginary-margin}
For a fixed pair, \(I=[\alpha_-,1]\), and \(\lambda\ge\lambda_->0\),
\[
 -\operatorname{Im}C\ge\gamma_C:=\lambda_-
 \min_{\alpha\in I}
 \frac{\nu_1^{-\alpha}\sin(\pi\alpha/2)}
      {\varrho^\alpha-1}>0.
\]
This is only separation from the quotient real axis.  It does not control
\(W_2-W_1\), and it does not remove the nonstrict real-part boundary at
\(\alpha=1\).
\end{corollary}

For stable computation after \(\alpha\) is isolated, the equivalent real
recovery
\[
 \kappa=-\frac{(\varrho^\alpha-1)\operatorname{Im}C}
               {\sin(\pi\alpha/2)}>0,\qquad
 \lambda=\nu_1^\alpha\kappa
\]
avoids accepting a nearly real complex value from \eqref{eq:lambda}; the
recovered \(\lambda\) must also be checked against \(\Lambda\).

\begin{remark}[Boundary and computation]
\[
 \lim_{\beta\to0^+}H_C(\beta)
 =\frac{2\operatorname{Im}C\log\varrho}{\pi}<0.
\]
At \(\alpha=0\), \(D=0\); no uniform conditioning exists as
\(\alpha_-\to0\) or \(\varrho\to1\).  Although model-consistent data have one
root, \(H_C\) need not be monotone on all of \(I\).  Nonideal data therefore
require complete root isolation or set inversion, not an unverified monotone
inverse.  The endpoint \(\alpha=1\) is admissible and is not rejected merely
because it lies on the boundary.
\end{remark}

\section{R1: joint forward-disk set}

Assume deterministic response disks
\begin{equation}
 |Z_{r,m}-\widetilde Z_{r,m}|\le\epsilon_{r,m}.
 \label{eq:response-disks}
\end{equation}
For every retained harmonic, pre-register the compact annulus
\[
 \Eset_m=\{q\in\C:q_{\min,m}\le|q|\le q_{\max,m}\},
 \qquad 0<q_{\min,m}<q_{\max,m}<\infty.
\]
For \((\beta,\ell)\in I\times\Lambda\), let
\[
 d_{r,m}(\beta,\ell)=\ell+(i\nu_{r,m})^\beta
\]
and
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal Q_{r,m}(\beta,\ell)=
 \mathcal D(d_{r,m}\widetilde Z_{r,m},
            |d_{r,m}|\epsilon_{r,m}).
 \label{eq:q-disk}
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Fixed-candidate forward-disk equivalence]
\label{thm:forward}
For fixed \((\beta,\ell)\), there is one \(q_m\in\Eset_m\), shared by all
rates, satisfying
\[
 \left|\widetilde Z_{r,m}-\frac{q_m}{d_{r,m}(\beta,\ell)}\right|
 \le\epsilon_{r,m}\quad\forall r
\]
if and only if
\begin{equation}
 \left(\bigcap_r\mathcal Q_{r,m}(\beta,\ell)\right)
 \cap\Eset_m\ne\varnothing.
 \label{eq:intersection}
\end{equation}
The joint zero-drift order set is
\begin{equation}
 \Aset_{\rm joint}=\proj_\beta
 \left\{(\beta,\ell)\in I\times\Lambda:
 \left(\bigcap_r\mathcal Q_{r,m}(\beta,\ell)\right)
 \cap\Eset_m\ne\varnothing\quad\forall m\in\Mset\right\}.
 \label{eq:joint}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Because \(d_{r,m}\ne0\),
\[
 \left|\widetilde Z_{r,m}-q_m/d_{r,m}\right|\le\epsilon_{r,m}
 \iff |d_{r,m}\widetilde Z_{r,m}-q_m|
 \le|d_{r,m}|\epsilon_{r,m}.
\]
The right side is membership in \(\mathcal Q_{r,m}\).  A shared coefficient
gives the intersection, and the annulus imposes compact nondegeneracy.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Independent complex morphology restitution]
If \(q_{r,m}=\bar q_m+\delta q_{r,m}\),
\(\bar q_m\in\Eset_m\), and each
\(|\delta q_{r,m}|\le\rho^q_{r,m}\) is an independent complex-disk variable,
then Theorem~\ref{thm:forward} remains exact after replacing the radius in
\eqref{eq:q-disk} by
\[
 |d_{r,m}|\epsilon_{r,m}+\rho^q_{r,m}.
\]
Correlated, real-only, or cross-harmonic restitution must retain its shared
variables; disk inflation is then only an outer enclosure.
\end{corollary}

\begin{theorem}[Positive independent morphology drift precludes point order]
\label{thm:drift-resolution}
Fix two rates and a known \(\lambda_0>0\).  Suppose exact zero-drift
responses are generated at \((\alpha_0,\lambda_0,q_0)\), where
the relevant single-harmonic morphology domain is the compact annulus
\(\Eset=\{q:q_{\min}\le|q|\le q_{\max}\}\),
\(0<q_{\min}<q_{\max}<\infty\), and
\(q_0\in\operatorname{int}\Eset\).  Allow independent morphology disks
of radii \(\rho_1,\rho_2\ge0\) with \(\rho_1+\rho_2>0\).  Fix \(h_0>0\) and put
\[
 c_r(\beta)=Z_r[\lambda_0+(i\nu_r)^\beta],\qquad
 F(\beta)=c_1(\beta)-c_2(\beta),
\]
and, on a compact relative neighborhood
\(J=I\cap[\alpha_0-h_0,\alpha_0+h_0]\), define
\[
 q_w(\beta)=\frac{\rho_2c_1(\beta)+\rho_1c_2(\beta)}
                  {\rho_1+\rho_2},\qquad
 L_F=\sup_J|F'|,\qquad L_w=\sup_J|q_w'|.
\]
With division by zero interpreted as \(+\infty\), every
\(\beta\in I\) satisfying
\[
 |\beta-\alpha_0|\le h:=\min\left\{
 h_0,\frac{\rho_1+\rho_2}{L_F},
 \frac{\operatorname{dist}(q_0,\Eset^c)}{L_w}
 \right\}
\]
is feasible.  In particular, the order projection contains a nondegenerate
relative neighborhood of \(\alpha_0\), one-sided when \(\alpha_0\) is an
endpoint.  Enlarging the damping prior while retaining \(\lambda_0\), or
adding response disks centred at the same exact responses with nonnegative
radii, can only enlarge this set.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
At the truth, \(c_1(\alpha_0)=c_2(\alpha_0)=q_0\).  The mean-value bound gives
\(|F(\beta)|\le L_F|\beta-\alpha_0|\le\rho_1+\rho_2\).  The weighted centre
satisfies
\[
 |q_w-c_1|=\frac{\rho_1}{\rho_1+\rho_2}|F|\le\rho_1,
 \quad
 |q_w-c_2|\le\rho_2,
\]
and \(|q_w(\beta)-q_0|\le L_w|\beta-\alpha_0|\) keeps it in \(\Eset\).
Thus \(q_w\) is a common witness.  Without the annulus, two-disk
intersection is exactly equivalent to \(|c_1-c_2|\le\rho_1+\rho_2\); with
the annulus the exact condition remains the three-way intersection and the
distance inequality alone is not sufficient.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Shared damping restitution]
Let
\[
 \lambda_r=\bar\lambda+\delta\lambda_r,\quad
 \bar\lambda\in\bar\Lambda,\quad
 |\delta\lambda_r|\le\rho^\lambda_r,\quad
 \lambda_r\in\Lambda_r\subset\R_{>0}.
\]
The same real \(\delta\lambda_r\) is shared across all harmonics at rate \(r\).
If \(\bar\lambda\) needs a unique nominal interpretation, impose a zero-mean
or anchored drift gauge.
\end{definition}

\begin{corollary}[Exact joint set with declared restitution]
Let
\[
 d^{\rm dr}_{r,m}
 =\bar\lambda+\delta\lambda_r+(i\nu_{r,m})^\beta,\qquad
 \mathcal Q^{\rm dr}_{r,m}
 =\mathcal D(d^{\rm dr}_{r,m}\widetilde Z_{r,m},
 |d^{\rm dr}_{r,m}|\epsilon_{r,m}+\rho^q_{r,m}).
\]
Then the exact order set under independent complex morphology disks and shared
real rate-indexed damping drift is
\[
 \proj_\beta\left\{(\beta,\bar\lambda,\delta\boldsymbol\lambda):
 \begin{array}{l}
 \beta\in I,\ \bar\lambda\in\bar\Lambda,
 |\delta\lambda_r|\le\rho^\lambda_r,
 \bar\lambda+\delta\lambda_r\in\Lambda_r,\\
 (\bigcap_r\mathcal Q^{\rm dr}_{r,m})\cap\Eset_m
 \ne\varnothing\quad\forall m\in\Mset
 \end{array}\right\}.
\]
A damping gauge is required to interpret \(\bar\lambda\) uniquely, but not to
form the conservative projection onto \(\beta\).
\end{corollary}

\begin{remark}[Certified algorithm boundary]
Fixed-parameter disk intersection with an annulus has finite planar geometry.
The current \texttt{certified\_set.py} implementation embeds numerical inputs
in containing Python-FLINT Arb/ACB balls, preserves immutable rate identifiers
and the anchored damping gauge, and uses three fast outer-exclusion tests
followed by a bounded Cartesian \(q\)-box cover.  Ordinary floating-point
geometry may only propose an inner witness; Arb/ACB inequalities verify it
before inclusion is issued.  Thus every issued outer exclusion or uniform
inner inclusion is a machine certificate.  Precision, \(q\)-box, branch, or
leaf-budget exhaustion remains unknown, so this certified core is not a
guaranteed finite exact projector.  The companion
\texttt{tube\_certificate.py} implements
Proposition~\ref{prop:exact-gram-krawczyk} and
Theorem~\ref{thm:selector-tube}: it uses an exact-rational Gram inverse,
Arb path inequalities, sealed per-interval evidence, and a replayed
alpha-only binary cover.  It issues only robust inner, closed inner, or
unknown results.
\end{remark}

\begin{assumption}[Certification coordinates]
\label{ass:cert-coordinates}
Every rate has an immutable identifier shared by all harmonics, and harmonic
frequencies are derived from one rate-indexed base-frequency vector rather
than independently sorted arrays.  Rate-dependent damping is written
\(\lambda_r=\bar\lambda+\delta\lambda_r\), with one preregistered reference
rate satisfying \(\delta\lambda_{r_0}=0\).  This anchor removes the translation
gauge between \(\bar\lambda\) and the offsets.  Before any norm or Gram margin
is evaluated, the protocol freezes a dimensionless numerical column scaling
for the mixed damping and morphology coordinates; every norm below acts on
that preregistered representation.  All numerical inputs are embedded in
containing real or complex balls.
\end{assumption}

\begin{lemma}[Anchored damping gauge is lossless]
\label{lem:anchored-damping}
For a fixed reference rate \(r_0\), the map
\[
 (\lambda_r)_r\longmapsto
 \left(\bar\lambda=\lambda_{r_0},\quad
 \delta\lambda_r=\lambda_r-\lambda_{r_0}\right)_r
\]
is a bijection between positive physical rate dampings and anchored
coordinates satisfying \(\delta\lambda_{r_0}=0\) and
\(\bar\lambda+\delta\lambda_r>0\).  It leaves every denominator
\(d_{r,m}\), hence the feasible set and its order projection, unchanged.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The displayed construction gives the anchored coordinates of any physical
damping vector.  Conversely,
\(\lambda_r=\bar\lambda+\delta\lambda_r\) is the unique inverse, because the
anchor forces \(\bar\lambda=\lambda_{r_0}\).  Substitution preserves every
model denominator exactly.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Rank test for the fully free damping centre map]
\label{lem:selector-rank}
Suppose \(R,M\ge1\) and every retained centre
\(\widetilde Z_{r,m}\) is nonzero.  In physical
damping coordinates, with all \(R\) physical rate dampings free, let the real
matrix \(B_{\rm full}\) represent the linear map
\[
 (a_1,\ldots,a_R,b_1,\ldots,b_M)\longmapsto
 (b_m-\widetilde Z_{r,m}a_r)_{r,m},
 \qquad a_r\in\R,\quad b_m\in\C.
\]
The complex outputs are stacked by real and imaginary parts.  For any
reference rate \(r_0\), \(B_{\rm full}\) has full column rank if and only if
there do not exist nonzero real numbers \(\kappa_r\) such that
\begin{equation}
 \widetilde Z_{r,m}=\kappa_r\widetilde Z_{r_0,m}
 \quad\text{for every }r,m.
 \label{eq:real-separable-response}
\end{equation}
The same condition holds after the invertible anchored coordinate change of
Assumption~\ref{ass:cert-coordinates}.  In particular,
\(2RM\ge R+2M\) is necessary in this fully free case.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
A null vector satisfies
\(b_m=\widetilde Z_{r,m}a_r\) for every \(r,m\).  If one \(a_r\) vanishes,
nonzero response centres force every \(b_m\), and then every \(a_r\), to
vanish.  Hence a nonzero null vector has all \(a_r\ne0\), and division by the
equation at \(r_0\) gives
\(\widetilde Z_{r,m}/\widetilde Z_{r_0,m}=a_{r_0}/a_r\), which is real and
independent of \(m\).  Conversely, if \eqref{eq:real-separable-response}
holds, \(a_{r_0}=1\), \(a_r=1/\kappa_r\), and
\(b_m=\widetilde Z_{r_0,m}\) form a nonzero null vector.  The anchored-to-
physical damping map in Lemma~\ref{lem:anchored-damping} is invertible, so it
cannot change rank.  The row-count condition follows immediately.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Exact elimination of fixed damping coordinates]
\label{cor:fixed-coordinate-elimination}
Before constructing a selector, fix every zero-width damping coordinate at its
declared value and absorb its contribution into the order-dependent centre
vector.  If \(p\) damping coordinates remain free, the resulting residual has
the exact reduced form
\[
 B_Jy-g_J(\beta),\qquad y\in\R^{p+2M}.
\]
Strict convexity requires only that \(B_J\) have full column rank; hence the
necessary row-count condition is \(2RM\ge p+2M\).  This elimination changes
neither the feasible fibres nor their order projection.  The real-separability
criterion of Lemma~\ref{lem:selector-rank} is the \(p=R\) special case; for a
general free-coordinate set \(J\), rank is checked on \(B_J\) itself.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Substitution of a fixed coordinate is an exact affine restriction.  Moving its
known term to \(g_J(\beta)\) leaves every residual value unchanged on that
restriction.  The row-count condition follows from the reduced dimensions;
full column rank of \(B_J\) remains a separate condition to be checked.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Gauge-reduced analytic least-squares selector]
\label{thm:analytic-selector}
After Corollary~\ref{cor:fixed-coordinate-elimination}, let
\(y\in\R^{p+2M}\) collect the \(p\) free anchored damping coordinates and the
real and imaginary parts of every \(q_m\).  Stack the centre residuals as
\begin{equation}
 \operatorname{vec}_{\R}
 \left(q_m-\widetilde Z_{r,m}
 [\bar\lambda+\delta\lambda_r+(i\nu_{r,m})^\beta]\right)_{r,m}
 =B_Jy-g_J(\beta),
 \label{eq:selector-residual}
\end{equation}
where all fixed contributions are included in \(g_J\), and \(B_J\) is
independent of \(\beta\).  Freeze positive weights in a diagonal matrix \(W\),
and suppose \(B_J\) has full column rank.  To lighten notation, write
\(B=B_J\) and \(g=g_J\) below.  Then
\[
 \Psi_\beta(y)=\tfrac12(By-g(\beta))^\top W(By-g(\beta))
\]
is strictly convex, with the unique global selector
\begin{equation}
 y_\star(\beta)=H^{-1}B^\top Wg(\beta),
 \qquad H=B^\top WB\succ0.
 \label{eq:analytic-selector}
\end{equation}
The map \(y_\star\) is the restriction of a real-analytic map on an open
neighbourhood of \(I\), and
\begin{align}
 y_\star'(\beta)&=H^{-1}B^\top Wg'(\beta),\label{eq:selector-first}\\
 y_\star''(\beta)&=H^{-1}B^\top Wg''(\beta),\label{eq:selector-second}
\end{align}
where, for each positive \(\nu\),
\[
 \frac{d^k}{d\beta^k}(i\nu)^\beta
 =(i\nu)^\beta(\log\nu+i\pi/2)^k,
 \qquad k=1,2.
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Positive diagonal \(W\) and full column rank of \(B\) give
\(v^\top Hv=\|Bv\|_W^2>0\) for every \(v\ne0\).  Thus the normal equation
\(Hy=B^\top Wg(\beta)\) has the unique solution
\eqref{eq:analytic-selector}, which is the unique global minimizer.  The
principal-branch formula is analytic for \(\nu>0\); \(B,W,H^{-1}\) are
constant, so differentiation gives \eqref{eq:selector-first}--
\eqref{eq:selector-second}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Explicit selector-conditioning bound]
\label{cor:selector-conditioning}
Let \(\mu=\lambda_{\min}(H)>0\),
\(\sigma=\|B^\top W\|_2\), and
\[
 L_{r,m}=\sqrt{(\log\nu_{r,m})^2+\pi^2/4}.
\]
For \(k=1,2\) and every \(\beta\in I\),
\begin{equation}
 \|y_\star^{(k)}(\beta)\|_2
 \le \frac{\sigma}{\mu}
 \left(\sum_{r,m}|\widetilde Z_{r,m}|^2
 \nu_{r,m}^{2\beta}L_{r,m}^{2k}\right)^{1/2}.
 \label{eq:selector-conditioning}
\end{equation}
The supremum over a closed interval \(A=[a,b]\) has the explicit upper bound
obtained by replacing \(\nu^{2\beta}\) with \(\nu^{2b}\) when \(\nu\ge1\)
and with \(\nu^{2a}\) when \(0<\nu<1\).
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Since \(H\) is symmetric positive definite,
\(\|H^{-1}\|_2=1/\mu\).  Equations
\eqref{eq:selector-first}--\eqref{eq:selector-second} and
submultiplicativity give
\(\|y_\star^{(k)}\|_2\le(\sigma/\mu)\|g^{(k)}\|_2\).
The squared Euclidean norm of the real--imaginary pair generated by one
complex derivative is
\(|\widetilde Z_{r,m}|^2\nu_{r,m}^{2\beta}L_{r,m}^{2k}\).
Summation proves \eqref{eq:selector-conditioning}; monotonicity of
\(\nu^{2\beta}\) gives the endpoint rule.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Exact rational spectral-margin certificate]
\label{prop:exact-spectral-margin}
In the implemented unweighted case \(W=I\), suppose the exact rational
Gram matrix \(H=B^\top B\) is nonsingular and let \(C=H^{-1}\).  Define
\[
 s_C=\lVert C\rVert_\infty,\qquad
 \mu_0=s_C^{-1},\qquad h_\infty=\lVert H\rVert_\infty.
\]
Then
\begin{equation}
 0<\mu_0\le\lambda_{\min}(H),\qquad
 \frac{\sigma^2}{\lambda_{\min}(H)^2}
 \le \frac{h_\infty}{\mu_0^2},
 \quad \sigma=\lVert B^\top\rVert_2.
 \label{eq:exact-spectral-baseline}
\end{equation}
Moreover, let \(0<\widehat\mu\in\mathbb Q\) and suppose exact rational
elimination gives
\[
 H-\widehat\mu I=LDL^\top,\qquad
 L_{ii}=1,\quad D=\operatorname{diag}(d_i),\quad d_i>0.
\]
Then
\[
 \widehat\mu<\lambda_{\min}(H),\qquad
 \frac{\sigma^2}{\lambda_{\min}(H)^2}
 <\frac{h_\infty}{\widehat\mu^2}.
\]
Thus a bounded rational bisection may sharpen the guaranteed baseline
\(\mu_0\); exhaustion of the refinement budget may fall back to \(\mu_0\)
without losing certification.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The matrix \(C\) is positive definite, so
\[
 \lambda_{\max}(C)=\lambda_{\min}(H)^{-1}
 \le\lVert C\rVert_\infty=s_C.
\]
This proves the first baseline inequality.  Since \(W=I\),
\(\sigma^2=\lambda_{\max}(BB^\top)=\lambda_{\max}(B^\top B)\)
on the nonzero spectrum, and
\(\lambda_{\max}(H)\le\lVert H\rVert_\infty\), proving the second.
For the refined claim, every nonzero \(x\) satisfies
\[
 x^\top(H-\widehat\mu I)x
 =(L^\top x)^\top D(L^\top x)>0.
\]
Hence \(H-\widehat\mu I\succ0\), which is equivalent to the asserted
strict eigenvalue inequality.  No floating-point eigensolver enters either
certificate.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Rate separation versus certification]
For the fully free damping case, Lemma~\ref{lem:selector-rank} is the
structural threshold, while \(\mu\) in
Corollary~\ref{cor:selector-conditioning} is its numerical margin.  As the
response array approaches the real-separable form
\eqref{eq:real-separable-response}, continuity forces
\(\mu\to0\), the selector derivatives can grow, and a fixed-width order tube
becomes harder to certify.  This is a conditioning statement, not an
information-theoretic lower bound.  Moreover, \(\mu\) and
\(\kappa_2(H)\) depend on the frozen units and column scaling of the mixed
damping/morphology coordinates; they are certification margins in those
coordinates, not intrinsic physical identifiability metrics.  Cross-dataset
comparisons require a preregistered nondimensional column scaling, after
which all spectral constants must be recomputed.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Quadratic upper collapse bound near real-separable arrays]
\label{prop:gram-collapse}
Use the fully free physical damping coordinates and the standard real
embedding of Lemma~\ref{lem:selector-rank}.  Let
\[
 \widetilde Z^0_{r,m}=\kappa_r\widetilde Z^0_{r_0,m},
 \qquad \kappa_r\in\R\setminus\{0\},
\]
and let \(\widetilde Z_{r,m}=\widetilde Z^0_{r,m}+\Delta Z_{r,m}\).
For \(B=B_{\rm full}(\widetilde Z)\), \(H=B^\top WB\), and
\(E_2=\sum_{r,m}|\Delta Z_{r,m}|^2\),
\[
 \lambda_{\min}(H)\le\lVert W\rVert_2 E_2.
\]
In the unweighted standard embedding, if \(H\succ0\) and \(E_2>0\), then
\[
 \kappa_2(H)\ge\frac{R}{E_2}.
\]
If \(H\) is singular or \(E_2=0\), the latter bound is interpreted as
\(+\infty\).  These constants change under anchored or rescaled coordinates.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The separable matrix \(B_0\) has a nonzero null vector by
Lemma~\ref{lem:selector-rank}.  For a unit null vector \(v_0\), with
\(E=B-B_0\),
\[
 \lambda_{\min}(H)\le v_0^\top Hv_0
 =\lVert W^{1/2}Ev_0\rVert_2^2
 \le\lVert W\rVert_2\lVert E\rVert_F^2
 =\lVert W\rVert_2E_2.
\]
For \(W=I\), every real or imaginary \(q_m\) column has squared norm \(R\);
hence \(\lambda_{\max}(H)\ge R\), and division by the first bound proves the
condition-number inequality.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Exact-Gram Krawczyk specialization]
\label{prop:exact-gram-krawczyk}
Suppose the stored response centres and frozen weights are interpreted as exact
rational numbers, the rank test passes, and \(C=H^{-1}\) is computed in exact
rational arithmetic.  Let \(\boldsymbol h(A)\) be a rigorous interval
extension of \(B^\top Wg(A)\).  For any point \(y^0\) and search box
\(\boldsymbol X\), the parameterized Krawczyk image of the normal equation is
\begin{align*}
 \boldsymbol K
 &=y^0-C(Hy^0-\boldsymbol h(A))
   +(I-CH)(\boldsymbol X-y^0)\\
 &=C\boldsymbol h(A).
\end{align*}
Thus \(I-CH=0\) exactly, the fixed-point contraction factor in the
\(y\)-direction is zero (this is not contraction in the parameter direction),
and
\(y_\star(\beta)\in\boldsymbol K\) for every \(\beta\in A\).  A replay checker
can verify the exact matrix identities before evaluating the original
inequalities; no approximate inverse is trusted.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Exact inversion gives \(CH=I\).  Substitution cancels both \(y^0\) terms and
the search-box term.  Since \(h(\beta)=B^\top Wg(\beta)\in\boldsymbol h(A)\),
\eqref{eq:analytic-selector} gives
\(y_\star(\beta)=Ch(\beta)\in C\boldsymbol h(A)\).  Equivalently, the
fixed-point map \(y\mapsto y-C(Hy-h(\beta))\) is the constant map
\(Ch(\beta)\).
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Quadratic-width predictor tube]
\label{cor:selector-taylor-tube}
Let \(A=[\beta_0-h,\beta_0+h]\subset(0,1]\), and let
\(M_j\ge\sup_{\beta\in A}|(y_\star''(\beta))_j|\).  Then every selector
component satisfies
\[
 (y_\star(\beta))_j\in
 (y_\star(\beta_0))_j+(y_\star'(\beta_0))_j(\beta-\beta_0)
 +[-M_jh^2/2,M_jh^2/2].
\]
Thus an affine predictor has a rigorously bounded \(O(h^2)\) remainder.  Direct
interval evaluation of \eqref{eq:analytic-selector} is also valid but may be
wider.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Apply Taylor's theorem with integral remainder componentwise and use
\(|\beta-\beta_0|\le h\).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Replayable second-order path enclosure]
The machine certificate applies Corollary~\ref{cor:selector-taylor-tube} both
to \(y_\star(\beta)=H^{-1}B^\top g(\beta)\) and to the correlation-preserving
residual
\[
 r_\star(\beta)=(BH^{-1}B^\top-I)g(\beta).
\]
Arb encloses the value and first derivative at the interval midpoint and the
second derivative over the complete interval.  Every component remainder is
rounded outward to an exact dyadic rational.  The final image is the
intersection of this Taylor enclosure and direct interval evaluation; since
the analytic path lies in both, the intersection is sound and can only be
tighter.  Replay reconstructs both source enclosures, the exact intersection,
and every original disk and annulus inequality.  The current implementation
uses \(W=I\); the theorem for a general frozen positive rational \(W\) is not
yet an implementation claim.
\end{remark}

\begin{definition}[Full and \(q\)-eliminated feasible sets]
\label{def:feasible-spaces}
Let \(\Theta\) be the declared compact anchored damping domain, including
positive physical-rate damping constraints.  Define
\[
 \widehat{\mathcal F}=
 \left\{(\beta,\theta,(q_m)_m):
 \begin{array}{l}
 \beta\in I,\ \theta\in\Theta,\ q_m\in\Eset_m,\\
 |q_m-d^{\rm dr}_{r,m}(\beta,\theta)\widetilde Z_{r,m}|
 \le |d^{\rm dr}_{r,m}(\beta,\theta)|\epsilon_{r,m}
      +\rho^q_{r,m}\quad\forall r,m
 \end{array}\right\},
\]
and let
\[
 \mathcal F=\proj_{(\beta,\theta)}\widehat{\mathcal F}.
\]
Thus \(\mathcal F\subseteq I\times\Theta\) is the exact parameter set after
existentially eliminating \(q\).  The branch tree below covers this same
domain \(I\times\Theta\), while a selector tube constructs points of
\(\widehat{\mathcal F}\).
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Certified analytic-selector inner tube]
\label{thm:selector-tube}
Let \(A\subset I\) be a closed order interval, and let the box
\(\boldsymbol Y(A)\) rigorously contain \(y_\star(\beta)\) for every
\(\beta\in A\), using either \eqref{eq:analytic-selector} or
Corollary~\ref{cor:selector-taylor-tube}.  Let
\(\boldsymbol\Theta_\star(A)\) be its projection onto the damping coordinates,
with fixed coordinates restored.  Suppose ball arithmetic proves the complete
physical inclusion
\(\boldsymbol\Theta_\star(A)\subseteq\Theta\), including all coupled
rate-damping positivity constraints.  Write
\(q_{\star,m}(\beta)\) and \(d^\star_{r,m}(\beta)\) for the corresponding
components and denominator along the analytic selector path.  Suppose,
additionally, that rigorous inclusion evaluations of these path expressions
prove, for every \(r,m\),
\begin{align}
 \sup_{\beta\in A}
 |q_{\star,m}(\beta)-d^\star_{r,m}(\beta)\widetilde Z_{r,m}|
 &\le
 \inf_{\beta\in A}
 (|d^\star_{r,m}(\beta)|\epsilon_{r,m}+\rho^q_{r,m}),
 \label{eq:tube-disk-margin}\\
 q_{\min,m}
 &\le\inf_{\beta\in A}|q_{\star,m}(\beta)|
 \le\sup_{\beta\in A}|q_{\star,m}(\beta)|
 \le q_{\max,m}.
 \label{eq:tube-annulus-margin}
\end{align}
Then
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{A\subseteq\proj_\beta\mathcal F},
 \label{eq:tube-inner-projection}
\end{equation}
with the feasible spaces of Definition~\ref{def:feasible-spaces}.  Strict
versions of every free-coordinate, disk, and annulus inequality define a
\emph{robust inner relative to the declared affine face}: coordinates frozen
to zero-width protocol intervals are restored exactly and are not claimed to
have ambient-space margin.  Rigorously established nonstrict inequalities
define a \emph{closed inner} certificate.  An unresolved sign yields no
inclusion claim.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For an arbitrary \(\beta\in A\), choose the unique selector
\(y_\star(\beta)\).  It belongs to \(\boldsymbol Y(A)\).  The coordinate
inclusions give admissible anchored dampings, while
\eqref{eq:tube-disk-margin} gives membership of the selected \(q_m\) in every
rate-indexed forward disk and \eqref{eq:tube-annulus-margin} gives membership
in \(\Eset_m\).  Hence the fibre of \(\widehat{\mathcal F}\) above every
\(\beta\in A\) is nonempty, which is exactly
\eqref{eq:tube-inner-projection}.  Strict inequalities additionally give the
reported positive margins.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Correlation-preserving evaluation]
The path expressions in Theorem~\ref{thm:selector-tube} need not be evaluated
over the full Cartesian product \(A\times\boldsymbol Y(A)\).  For the central
least-squares selector, the residual is exactly
\[
 r_\star(\beta)=(BH^{-1}B^\top W-I)g(\beta),
\]
so evaluating this expression directly preserves useful dependence on
\(\beta\) while remaining a rigorous enclosure.  Replacing it by an
independent box evaluation over \(\boldsymbol Y(A)\) is also sound but can be
strictly wider.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Selector semantics and failure]
The uniqueness in Theorem~\ref{thm:analytic-selector} concerns only the frozen
least-squares selector.  It implies neither uniqueness of a physically feasible
nuisance vector nor uniqueness of the fractional order.  If the Gram matrix is
singular, the selector leaves its prior box, or one inequality in
Theorem~\ref{thm:selector-tube} is unresolved, the tube returns unknown.  In
particular, failure of this sufficient construction is not an outer-exclusion
certificate.  Equating \(q_m\) to an arbitrarily selected disk centre would be
unsound as a general selector: two disks can overlap in a lens containing
neither centre.
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[Parameter-box disk hull and core]
\label{thm:hull-core}
Let \(B\) be a compact box in the anchored certification coordinates and write
\[
 c_{r,m}(x)=d_{r,m}(x)\widetilde Z_{r,m},\qquad
 s_{r,m}(x)=|d_{r,m}(x)|\epsilon_{r,m}+\rho^q_{r,m}.
\]
Choose \(x^0\in B\) and define the exact mathematical reference
\(c^0_{r,m}=c_{r,m}(x^0)\); it need not be machine-representable.  Suppose
rigorous ball evaluation encloses \(c^0_{r,m}\) in a containing complex ball
and certifies bounds
\[
 e_{r,m}\ge\sup_{x\in B}|c_{r,m}(x)-c^0_{r,m}|,\quad
 s^-_{r,m}\le\inf_{x\in B}s_{r,m}(x),\quad
 s^+_{r,m}\ge\sup_{x\in B}s_{r,m}(x).
\]
Then
\begin{align}
 \bigcup_{x\in B}\mathcal Q_{r,m}(x)
 &\subseteq
 \mathcal D(c^0_{r,m},e_{r,m}+s^+_{r,m})
 =:\mathcal Q^{\rm out}_{r,m}(B),\label{eq:outer-disk}\\
 \mathcal D(c^0_{r,m},s^-_{r,m}-e_{r,m})
 =:\mathcal Q^{\rm in}_{r,m}(B)
 &\subseteq
 \bigcap_{x\in B}\mathcal Q_{r,m}(x),\label{eq:inner-disk}
\end{align}
whenever \(s^-_{r,m}-e_{r,m}\ge0\) is certified.  If its sign is
undetermined, no inner disk may be issued.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For \(q\in\mathcal Q_{r,m}(x)\),
\[
 |q-c^0_{r,m}|
 \le |q-c_{r,m}(x)|+|c_{r,m}(x)-c^0_{r,m}|
 \le s^+_{r,m}+e_{r,m},
\]
which proves \eqref{eq:outer-disk}.  Conversely, if
\(|q-c^0_{r,m}|\le s^-_{r,m}-e_{r,m}\), then for every \(x\in B\),
\[
 |q-c_{r,m}(x)|
 \le |q-c^0_{r,m}|+|c^0_{r,m}-c_{r,m}(x)|
 \le (s^-_{r,m}-e_{r,m})+e_{r,m}
 =s^-_{r,m}\le s_{r,m}(x),
\]
which proves \eqref{eq:inner-disk}.
In a machine proof, every comparison involving \(c^0_{r,m}\) is evaluated
against its entire containing ball; this can only make the test more
conservative.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Finite Cartesian \(q\)-box exclusion certificate]
\label{prop:qbox-cover}
Fix a harmonic \(m\).  Let \(C_{r,m}\subset\C\) be a containing complex
center ball and let \(R_{r,m}\) be an upper bound for the radius of
\(\mathcal Q^{\rm out}_{r,m}(B)\).  Suppose a finite exact partition of
\([-q_{\max,m},q_{\max,m}]^2\) is constructed, and every Cartesian cell
\(P\) satisfies at least one strictly certified condition
\[
 \inf_{q\in P}|q|>q_{\max,m},\qquad
 \sup_{q\in P}|q|<q_{\min,m},\qquad\text{or}\qquad
 \inf_{\substack{q\in P\\c\in C_{r,m}}}|q-c|>R_{r,m}
 \quad\text{for some }r.
\]
Then
\[
 \left(\bigcap_r\mathcal Q^{\rm out}_{r,m}(B)\right)
 \cap\Eset_m=\varnothing.
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The square contains the complete annulus.  By hypothesis, every point of
every partition cell is either outside the closed annulus or outside at least
one outer disk, so no point can belong to their joint intersection.  Strict
inequalities preserve all disk and annulus boundary points; an unresolved
cell therefore cannot be discarded.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Certified projection sandwich]
\label{thm:projection-sandwich}
Let a finite branch tree cover the initial compact domain without gaps and
classify each leaf \(B\) as follows:
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
O:&\text{for some \(m\), the outer disks in \eqref{eq:outer-disk}
have certified empty intersection with \(\Eset_m\);}\\
I:&\text{for every \(m\), one rigorously verified witness lies in
all inner disks in \eqref{eq:inner-disk} and in \(\Eset_m\);}\\
U:&\text{neither statement has been proved.}
\end{array}
\]
Let \(\mathcal T\) be any collection of order intervals certified by
Theorem~\ref{thm:selector-tube}.  With the exact \(q\)-eliminated parameter set
\(\mathcal F\) from Definition~\ref{def:feasible-spaces}, let
\[
 \Aset_{\rm in}=\left(\bigcup_{B\in I}\proj_\beta B\right)
 \cup\left(\bigcup_{A\in\mathcal T}A\right),\qquad
 \Aset_{\rm out}=\bigcup_{B\in I\cup U}\proj_\beta B,
\]
where every \(A\in\mathcal T\) lies in the initial order domain, then
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{\Aset_{\rm in}\subseteq\proj_\beta\mathcal F
 \subseteq\Aset_{\rm out}}.
 \label{eq:sandwich}
\end{equation}
Only when \(U=\varnothing\) may the computed projection be called exact.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Theorem~\ref{thm:hull-core} makes every \(O\) leaf disjoint from
\(\mathcal F\) and every \(I\) leaf a subset of \(\mathcal F\).  Since
\(I\cup O\cup U\) covers the same initial \(I\times\Theta\) domain from
Definition~\ref{def:feasible-spaces}, removing only \(O\) leaves cannot remove
a feasible point, which gives the outer inclusion in
\eqref{eq:sandwich}.  Projecting the \(I\) leaves and
applying \eqref{eq:tube-inner-projection} to every member of \(\mathcal T\)
gives the inner inclusion.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Finite-resource completeness]
Ball arithmetic certifies every issued inequality.  It does not remove
dependency inflation or guarantee finite decisions at tangencies.  Precision
exhaustion, branch or leaf budgets, degenerate boundaries, and unsupported
input domains must remain \(U\).  Positive-separation infeasible boxes are
asymptotically excludable under convergent inclusion functions; finite
classification of every boundary box is neither assumed nor claimed.
\end{remark}

\section{R2: finite-record data-to-disk theorem}

Let \(t_n\) be samples in one rate window \([a,b]\), \(w_n>0\),
\(\sum_nw_n=1\).  For discrete WLS, also freeze exact integer sample indices
\(j_n\in[j_a,j_b)\).  The endpoints span \(N\) complete detected R--R
intervals.  Put \(T=b-a\) and \(\widehat\omega_r=2\pi N/T\).  The discrete
estimator uses the exact nominal index phase
\[
 \widehat\phi_n=2\pi N\frac{j_n-j_a}{j_b-j_a};
\]
it does not reconstruct that ratio through rounded binary64 seconds.
Timestamp departures from the nominal clock enter the sampling-error budget.
The continuous-lock-in branch instead uses
\(\widehat\phi(t)=\widehat\omega_r(t-a)\).  The window is released only after
its right R-peak anchor arrives; it is delayed-causal, not zero-delay.
No rate window is independently rephased or rescaled.  For estimator-basis
harmonics \(k\in\Kset\), define
\[
 \Psi_{n,k}=e^{ik\widehat\phi_n},\quad
 W=\operatorname{diag}(w_n),\quad G=\Psi^*W\Psi.
\]
Assume \(G\succ0\).  Let \(\widehat H_m\ne0\) abbreviate the independently
calibrated \(\widehat H_{r,m}\) for the current rate/window under one fixed
causal front end (identity preprocessing means \(\widehat H_m=1\)).  It may
vary with physical frequency and is not a free rate-specific morphology gain.
Use
\begin{equation}
 \widetilde{\boldsymbol C}=G^{-1}\Psi^*W\boldsymbol y,\qquad
 \widetilde Z_m=\widetilde C_m/\widehat H_m.
 \label{eq:wls}
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Conditional finite-sample enclosure]
\label{thm:wls}
If \(\boldsymbol y=\Psi\operatorname{diag}(\widehat H_k)
\boldsymbol Z+\boldsymbol r\) and an independent protocol
guarantees \(\|\boldsymbol r\|_W\le\bar r\), then
\begin{equation}
 |\widetilde Z_m-Z_m|
 \le\frac{\sqrt{(G^{-1})_{mm}}}{|\widehat H_m|}\,\bar r
 =:\epsilon_m.
 \label{eq:wls-bound}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
\[
 \widetilde{\boldsymbol C}
 -\operatorname{diag}(\widehat H_k)\boldsymbol Z
 =G^{-1}\Psi^*W\boldsymbol r.
\]
The squared weighted operator norm of row \(m\) is
\[
 e_m^*G^{-1}\Psi^*W\Psi G^{-1}e_m=(G^{-1})_{mm}.
\]
Cauchy--Schwarz followed by division by \(|\widehat H_m|\) gives
\eqref{eq:wls-bound}.
\end{proof}

To make \(\bar r\) operational, let the record start at \(t_0\), let a rate
segment switch at \(t_{\rm switch}\ge t_0\), set
\(S=(t_{\rm switch}-t_0)/\tau_\star\), and impose \(z(t_0)=z_0\).  Let
\(u_{\rm pre}\) act on \([t_0,t_{\rm switch})\) and \(u_0\) after the switch.
Let the estimation window start after burn-in \(b_0\), and put
\(s_n=(t_n-t_{\rm switch})/\tau_\star\in[b_0,b_0+T/\tau_\star]\) and
\[
 M_{\alpha,\lambda}(s)=E_\alpha(-\lambda s^\alpha),\qquad
 k_{\alpha,\lambda}(s)=s^{\alpha-1}
 E_{\alpha,\alpha}(-\lambda s^\alpha).
\]
Assume the pre-switch and new nominal inputs obey
\(\|u_{\rm pre}\|_\infty\le U_{\rm pre}\) and
\(\|u_0\|_\infty\le U_0\).  The finite-lower-limit and switch-memory
envelopes are
\begin{align}
 B_{\rm hist}(s)
 &=\left(|z_0|+\frac{U_0}{\lambda}\right)
   M_{\alpha,\lambda}(S+s),\label{eq:hist}\\
 B_{\rm dwell}(s)
 &=\frac{U_{\rm pre}+U_0}{\lambda}
   [M_{\alpha,\lambda}(s)-M_{\alpha,\lambda}(S+s)].
 \label{eq:dwell}
\end{align}
If there is additional unmodelled pre-record Caputo history, an independently
calibrated term \(C_{\rm pre}M_{\alpha,\lambda}(S+s)\) is mandatory.  For a
nonidentity causal front end with impulse response \(h\), replace each
time-domain envelope \(B\) by \(|h|*B\) before taking its weighted norm.

Define a separate operational beatwise R--R phase by linear interpolation
between detected R peaks.  It is not the WLS basis above: its deviation from
the fixed nominal phase is propagated as an HRV perturbation.  Let the total
certified phase error be \(\Delta_\phi(s)\), including the observable warp,
R-time error, and any claimed latent-phase discrepancy.  If
\(|R_j-\widehat R_j|\le\Delta_R\), the detection part can be bounded within
beat \(j\) by
\[
 \Delta_{\phi,R,j}\le
 \frac{2\pi\Delta_R}{\widehat T_j-2\Delta_R},\qquad
 \widehat T_j>2\Delta_R.
\]
With \(d(x)=2\sin(\min\{x,\pi\}/2)\) and independently bounded input
harmonics \(|q_k|\le\overline Q_k\), require either a finite forcing basis or
\(\sum_k\overline Q_k<\infty\), and put
\begin{align}
 \Delta U(s)&=\sum_{k\in\mathbb Z}\overline Q_k
 d(|k|\Delta_\phi(s)),\label{eq:phase-input}\\
 B_{\rm HRV}(s)&=\int_0^s k_{\alpha,\lambda}(s-v)
 \Delta U(v)\,dv.\label{eq:hrv}
\end{align}
When \(\Delta U\le\overline{\Delta U}\), complete monotonicity gives
\[
 B_{\rm HRV}(s)\le
 \frac{\overline{\Delta U}}{\lambda}
 [1-M_{\alpha,\lambda}(s)].
\]
Thus HRV is propagated through the fractional dynamics; it is not merely an
output-phasor rotation.

Any remaining rate-specific anchor mismatch that is not already included in
\(\Delta_\phi\) must have an independently certified output-residual envelope
\(B_{\rm phase}(s)\), with disjoint provenance and
\[
 \bar r_{\rm phase}\ge
 \left(\sum_n w_n B_{\rm phase}(s_n)^2\right)^{1/2}.
\]

For the discrete WLS estimator, define
\[
 a_m=\sqrt{(G^{-1})_{mm}},\qquad
 h_{mk}=e_m^*G^{-1}\Psi^*W\boldsymbol\psi_k.
\]
A sufficient coefficient-level radius is
\begin{align}
 \epsilon_{r,m}
 &=\frac{a_m}{|\widehat H_m|}
   (\bar r_{\rm hist}+\bar r_{\rm dwell}+\bar r_{\rm HRV}
   +\bar r_{\rm phase}+\bar r_{\rm sample}
   +\bar r_{\rm model}+\bar r_{\rm noise})
   \notag\\
 &\quad+\epsilon^{\rm omitted}_{r,m}
   +\epsilon^{\rm delay}_{r,m}
   +\epsilon^{\rm quad}_{r,m}
   +\epsilon^{\rm filt-init}_{r,m},
 \label{eq:total-radius}
\end{align}
where, for \(j\in\{\mathrm{hist},\mathrm{dwell},\mathrm{HRV}\}\),
\begin{equation}
 \bar r_j\ge
 \sup_{(\alpha,\lambda)\in I\times\Lambda}
 \left(\sum_n w_n B_j(s_n)^2\right)^{1/2}.
 \label{eq:dynamic-budget}
\end{equation}
The remaining terms require the following independent bounds.

\paragraph{Window leakage and harmonic tail.}
If \(\sum_k\overline{|H_{r,k}|}\,\overline Z_{r,k}<\infty\), then
\[
 \epsilon^{\rm omitted}_{r,m}\le
 \sum_{k\notin\Kset}
 \frac{|h_{mk}|\overline{|H_{r,k}|}}{|\widehat H_m|}
 \overline Z_{r,k}.
\]
For a rectangular single-harmonic window of phase length
\(L=\widehat\omega_rT\),
\[
 h_{mk}=e^{i(k-m)L/2}
 \operatorname{sinc}((k-m)L/2).
\]
The nominal leakage vanishes when \(L=2\pi N\), but transient, HRV, and model
errors do not.

\paragraph{Sampling and interpolation.}
For sample-time error \(\Delta t_n\), ADC step \(\Delta_{\rm ADC}\), and
independently bounded interpolation error,
\[
 \bar r_{\rm sample}\ge
 \left[\sum_nw_n
 (M_1\Delta t_n+\Delta_{\rm ADC}/2+\epsilon_{{\rm interp},n}
  +\epsilon_{{\rm aa},n})^2
 \right]^{1/2}.
\]
If a continuous lock-in integral is instead approximated by the trapezoidal
rule, freeze the decomposition
\[
 y(t_n)=x_{\rm nom}^{\rm ret}(t_n)+e_n.
\]
Here \(x_{\rm nom}^{\rm ret}\) is exactly the retained-harmonic, nominal-front-
end reconstruction whose continuous coefficient defines the target; every
dynamic, acquisition, noise, and model term handled by samplewise bounds is
placed in \(e_n\).  Bound the trapezoidal functional of \(e_n\) by its declared
sample envelopes, and add the following quadrature remainder only for the
nominal reconstruction:
\[
 \epsilon^{\rm quad}_{r,m}\le
 \frac{1}{12T|\widehat H_m|}\sum_j\Delta t_j^3M_{2,j},
 \quad
 M_{2,j}\ge\sup_{[t_j,t_{j+1}]}
 \left|(x_{\rm nom}^{\rm ret})''
 -2im\widehat\omega_r(x_{\rm nom}^{\rm ret})'
 -(m\widehat\omega_r)^2x_{\rm nom}^{\rm ret}\right|.
\]
Using the second derivative of the actual signal here would mix two
incompatible decompositions: its continuous residual is not controlled by
sample values alone.  The constants \(M_1,M_{2,j}\) cannot be certified from the same samples
without an anti-aliasing, bandwidth, derivative, or interval-reconstruction
assumption.

\paragraph{Front-end delay, noise, and model discrepancy.}
If
\[
 \frac{H_{r,m}}{\widehat H_m}=(1+\delta_a)e^{-i\delta_\psi},\quad
 |\delta_a|\le a_H,\quad
 |\delta_\psi|\le |m|\widehat\omega_r\Delta_\tau+\Delta_{\psi,0},
\]
then
\[
 \epsilon^{\rm delay}_{r,m}\le\overline Z_{r,m}
 \left[a_H+(1+a_H)d(|m|\widehat\omega_r\Delta_\tau+
 \Delta_{\psi,0})\right].
\]
A common phase angle may be absorbed into \(q_m\); a common delay in seconds
may not, because its phase depends on heart rate.  Deterministic noise obeys
\[
 \|\boldsymbol v\|_W\le\bar v_W
 \Longrightarrow
 \bar r_{\rm noise}:=\bar v_W,
\]
for discrete WLS; this discrete norm needs its own calibration.  A continuous
\(L_2\) bound alone cannot control point samples without an independently
certified sampling or reconstruction inequality.  For the exact continuous
lock-in functional, however, Cauchy--Schwarz gives directly
\[
 \left|\frac1T\int_a^b v(t)e^{-im\widehat\omega_rt}\,dt\right|
 \le\frac{N_2}{\sqrt T},
 \qquad \|v\|_{L_2(a,b)}\le N_2.
\]
The trapezoidal implementation then also needs the separately certified
quadrature contract stated above.  A Gaussian or RMS SNR gives a confidence
disk, not a deterministic disk.  If the causal filter starts at \(t_0\), its
pre-start input is bounded by \(Y_{\max}\), and its impulse response is \(h\),
define the initialization envelope
\[
 B_{\rm init}(t):=
 Y_{\max}\int_{t-t_0}^{\infty}|h(s)|\,ds.
\]
The extraction-functional contract must then be respected.  For exact
continuous lock-in,
\[
 \epsilon^{{\rm filt-init},{\rm cont}}_{r,m}\le
 \frac{1}{T|\widehat H_m|}\int_a^bB_{\rm init}(t)\,dt.
\]
For discrete WLS, with the row norm \(a_m\) of
Theorem~\ref{thm:wls},
\[
 \epsilon^{{\rm filt-init},{\rm WLS}}_{r,m}\le
 \frac{a_m}{|\widehat H_m|}
 \left(\sum_nw_nB_{\rm init}(t_n)^2\right)^{1/2}.
\]
For the trapezoidal sample functional with internally derived weights
\(\omega_n^{\rm trap}\),
\[
 \epsilon^{{\rm filt-init},{\rm trap}}_{r,m}\le
 \frac{1}{T|\widehat H_m|}
 \sum_n\omega_n^{\rm trap}B_{\rm init}(t_n).
\]
The last bound concerns the sampled initialization residual; any continuous
quadrature remainder remains governed by the separately frozen decomposition
above.
The model term \(\bar r_{\rm model}\) must likewise be bounded independently;
choosing it from the residual of the same fitted surrogate is circular.

\begin{theorem}[Conditional finite-record data-to-disk map]
Under the dimensionless model, delayed-causal phase protocol, calibrated
front end, and every independent deterministic bound in
\eqref{eq:hist}--\eqref{eq:total-radius},
\[
 |\widetilde Z_{r,m}-Z_{r,m}|\le\epsilon_{r,m}.
\]
Consequently the response disk in \eqref{eq:response-disks} has guaranteed
coverage.  The sum is a valid outer disk; it need not be the smallest disk
when errors are correlated.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Equations \eqref{eq:hist} and \eqref{eq:dwell} follow by splitting the scalar
Mittag--Leffler convolution at the recording and rate-switch boundaries.
Equation \eqref{eq:hrv} is the same convolution applied to the bounded input
perturbation \eqref{eq:phase-input}.  The remaining displayed inequalities
bound linear extraction, leakage, sampling, calibration, noise, and
initialization terms.  The triangle inequality and Theorem~\ref{thm:wls}
give \eqref{eq:total-radius}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Elementary uniform Mittag--Leffler envelope]
\label{prop:ml-envelope}
Let \(I=[\alpha_-,\alpha_+]\subset(0,1]\),
\(\Lambda=[\lambda_-,\lambda_+]\subset\mathbb R_{>0}\), and
\[
 p_I(t)=
 \begin{cases}
 0,&t=0,\\
 t^{\alpha_+},&0<t\le1,\\
 t^{\alpha_-},&t\ge1,
 \end{cases}
 \qquad
 R_I(t)=\frac{1}{1+\lambda_-p_I(t)}.
\]
Then, uniformly for \((\alpha,\lambda)\in I\times\Lambda\) and \(t\ge0\),
\begin{equation}
 0<E_\alpha(-\lambda t^\alpha)\le R_I(t).
 \label{eq:ml-rational-envelope}
\end{equation}
Consequently,
\begin{align}
 B_{\rm hist}(s)
 &\le
 \left(|z_0|+\frac{U_0}{\lambda_-}+C_{\rm pre}\right)R_I(S+s),
 \label{eq:cert-hist-envelope}\\
 B_{\rm dwell}(s)
 &\le
 \frac{U_{\rm pre}+U_0}{\lambda_-}R_I(s).
 \label{eq:cert-dwell-envelope}
\end{align}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For \(0<\alpha<1\), Simon's uniform bound gives
\[
 E_\alpha(-x)\le
 \frac{1}{1+x/\Gamma(1+\alpha)},\qquad x\ge0
 \]
\cite{simon2014}.  Log-convexity of \(\Gamma\), together with
\(\Gamma(1)=\Gamma(2)=1\), gives \(\Gamma(1+\alpha)\le1\), hence
\(E_\alpha(-x)\le(1+x)^{-1}\).  At \(\alpha=1\),
\(E_1(-x)=e^{-x}\le(1+x)^{-1}\).  By construction,
\(p_I(t)\le t^\alpha\) and \(\lambda_-\le\lambda\), proving the upper
bound.  Pollard's complete monotonicity theorem gives positivity and
monotone decrease of \(E_\alpha(-x)\) \cite{pollard1948}.  Applying these
facts to \eqref{eq:hist}--\eqref{eq:dwell}, and dropping the nonnegative
subtracted dwell term, proves the two envelopes.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Replayable WLS Gram margin]
\label{prop:r2-gram-margin}
Suppose complex balls \(G_{jk}\) contain every entry of the Hermitian WLS Gram
matrix.  Let \(\underline g_{jj}\) be a lower bound for its real diagonal,
\(\overline g_{jj}\) an upper bound, and
\(\overline a_{jk}\ge |G_{jk}|\) for \(j\ne k\).  Define
\[
 g_-=\min_j\left(\underline g_{jj}
       -\sum_{k\ne j}\overline a_{jk}\right),\qquad
 g_+=\max_j\left(\overline g_{jj}
       +\sum_{k\ne j}\overline a_{jk}\right).
\]
If \(g_->0\), then
\[
 G\succ0,\qquad
 \lambda_{\min}(G)\ge g_-,\qquad
 \lambda_{\max}(G)\le g_+,\qquad
 \kappa_2(G)\le\frac{g_+}{g_-},
\]
and every WLS row factor satisfies
\[
 \sqrt{(G^{-1})_{mm}}\le g_-^{-1/2}.
\]
If \(g_->0\) cannot be proved, this test is inconclusive rather than a
singularity certificate.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The first two spectral inclusions are the Hermitian Gershgorin theorem applied
with outward-containing entries.  Positivity of every lower endpoint implies
positive definiteness.  Spectral inversion then gives
\((G^{-1})_{mm}\le\lambda_{\max}(G^{-1})=1/\lambda_{\min}(G)\).
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Verified shifted Hermitian LDL* fallback]
\label{prop:r2-gram-ldl}
Let \(\boldsymbol G_{jk}\), \(j\ge k\), be lower-triangular complex balls
containing the corresponding entries of one exact Hermitian Gram matrix \(G\);
the upper triangle is reconstructed by conjugate symmetry.  Let \(\mu>0\) be
rational and apply unpivoted ball LDL* elimination to
\(\boldsymbol G-\mu I\).  At each step retain the real
projection of the pivot only after its imaginary ball is verified to contain
zero.  If every real pivot ball has a strictly positive lower endpoint, then
\[
 G-\mu I\succ0,\qquad \lambda_{\min}(G)>\mu.
\]
Together with the Gershgorin upper bound \(g_+\) from
Proposition~\ref{prop:r2-gram-margin}, this gives
\[
 \kappa_2(G)<\frac{g_+}{\mu},\qquad
 \sqrt{(G^{-1})_{mm}}<\mu^{-1/2}.
\]
Failure of any pivot sign is inconclusive.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Inductively, ball operations contain the unique exact unpivoted LDL* factors
of the represented Hermitian matrix.  Every exact Schur-complement pivot is
real; the imaginary containment check guards that invariant before the real
projection is used.  Strict positivity of all exact pivots proves
\(G-\mu I\succ0\) by the Hermitian Sylvester criterion.  The spectral bounds
then follow as in Proposition~\ref{prop:r2-gram-margin}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Exact-integer-payload WLS numerical enclosure]
\label{prop:exact-payload-wls}
Freeze a selected digital payload \(d_n\in\mathbb Z\),
\(n=1,\ldots,n_s\), integer baseline
\(b_{\rm ADC}\), positive binary-rational gain \(g_{\rm ADC}\), integer sample
indices \(j_n\), window endpoints \(a<b\), complete RR count \(N\), and the
contiguous two-sided basis \(\Kset\).  Interpret
\[
 y_n=\frac{d_n-b_{\rm ADC}}{g_{\rm ADC}},\qquad
 w_n=\frac1{n_s},\qquad
 \phi_n=2\pi N\frac{j_n-a}{b-a}
\]
exactly.  Suppose complex ball arithmetic directly encloses every
\(e^{ik\phi_n}\), accumulates containing balls
\(\boldsymbol G\ni\Psi^*W\Psi\) and
\(\boldsymbol b\ni\Psi^*Wy\), and a verified linear solve returns
\(\boldsymbol C\) containing the exact solution.  Let the frozen front-end
value \(\widehat H_m\ne0\) be interpreted as an exact binary complex rational,
and let
\[
 \boldsymbol Z^{\rm func}_m=\boldsymbol C_m/\widehat H_m,\qquad
 \rho_m\ge\sup\{|z-\widetilde Z_m|:
 z\in\boldsymbol Z^{\rm func}_m\}
\]
be an outward dyadic bound.  Then the exact mathematical sample-functional
coefficient satisfies
\[
 |Z^{\rm func}_m-\widetilde Z_m|\le\rho_m.
\]
Consequently \(\rho_m\) is a sufficient numerical floor for the sampling
radius of a disk centred at the stored binary64 \(\widetilde Z_m\).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The integer and binary-rational inputs are exact.  Inclusion isotonicity of
Arb/ACB operations \cite{johansson2017} contains every exponential, finite
sum, and product in the
displayed exact \(G\) and \(b\).  A successful verified solve contains the
unique exact \(C\); division by the nonzero exact \(\widehat H_m\) contains
\(Z^{\rm func}_m\).  The final complex-ball modulus and outward dyadic comparison give
the asserted disk inequality.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Scope of the exact-payload enclosure]
Proposition~\ref{prop:exact-payload-wls} closes phase evaluation,
\(G,b\) accumulation, linear-solve, and stored-centre rounding for the frozen
sample functional.  It does not bound sensor error, ADC-gain calibration
uncertainty, R-annotation error, subsampling aliasing, front-end calibration
uncertainty, or model discrepancy.  Those remain independent R2 terms.
In particular, \(Z^{\rm func}_m\) is not the true model response \(Z_m\);
coverage of the latter requires the complete remaining R2 budget.
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[Machine-certified partial R2 component map]
\label{thm:r2-component-certificate}
Fix exact binary-rational representations of the sample times, protocol
intervals, and independently sourced primitive bounds.  Normalize declared
positive weights exactly for discrete WLS; for continuous lock-in, derive the
unique nonuniform composite-trapezoid weights internally from those exact
timestamps.  For the dynamic residual, require either an
exactly identity front end or independently certified per-sample output
envelopes containing \((|h|*B_{\rm hist})(s_n)\) and
\((|h|*B_{\rm dwell})(s_n)\).  For estimator extraction, use either the
discrete WLS map of \eqref{eq:wls}, or a complete-cycle continuous lock-in
approximated with the exact nonuniform composite-trapezoid weights; in the
latter case the continuous harmonic Gram is \(I\), and the panelwise
quadrature remainder is added separately with decomposition target
\(x_{\rm nom}^{\rm ret}\) frozen as above.

Suppose ball arithmetic proves either
Proposition~\ref{prop:r2-gram-margin} or
Proposition~\ref{prop:r2-gram-ldl} for the discrete path (or verifies the
exact \(G=I\) continuous path) and evaluates
the applicable dynamic output envelopes, sampling norm, quadrature remainder,
and front-end delay expression with outward rounding.  Then the resulting
history, dwell, sampling, quadrature, delay, Gram, condition-number, and
extraction-amplification upper bounds are simultaneous deterministic
component bounds for the frozen window.

A certificate that binds the complete input manifest, evidence hashes,
backend and precision, outward rational enclosures, and a canonical digest
can be independently replayed by reconstructing every derived quantity.
Failure of a source contract, a positive Gram lower bound, ball comparison,
or a resource limit has only \texttt{UNKNOWN}/\texttt{NOT\_CERTIFIABLE}
semantics.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Proposition~\ref{prop:ml-envelope} bounds the two model-input memory sequences
pointwise.  Identity propagation preserves them; otherwise the separately
certified convolved envelopes are used.  Positive normalized weights and
monotonicity of the square root preserve their weighted-norm bounds.
Propositions~\ref{prop:r2-gram-margin} and~\ref{prop:r2-gram-ldl} supply the
discrete-WLS coefficient amplification.  Orthogonality over a complete
integer number of cycles gives
\(G=I\) for the continuous lock-in path, while requiring the exact composite
weights binds its sampled implementation.  The sample bound follows from the
pointwise triangle inequality, and the trapezoid term follows panelwise from
the Peano-kernel error bound.  Finally,
\(|e^{-i\theta}-1|=2|\sin(\theta/2)|\), capped by \(2\), gives the declared
delay expression.  All operations are inclusion isotone under outward ball
arithmetic.  Recalculation from the bound input rather than trust in stored
derived fields proves replay.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Frozen-selector R2-to-T1 margin transfer]
\label{thm:r2-t1-transfer}
Let one T1 certificate provide a selector path
\((d^\star_{r,m}(\alpha),q^\star_m(\alpha))\) on a closed interval \(A\)
and certified old disk margins
\[
 \mu_{r,m}(\alpha)=
 |d^\star_{r,m}|\epsilon_{r,m}+\rho^q_{r,m}
 -|q^\star_m-d^\star_{r,m}\widetilde Z_{r,m}|
 \ge\underline\mu_{r,m}\ge0.
\]
Keep this old path fixed.  Consider an independently certified family of new
R2 disks
\[
 \widetilde Z'=\widetilde Z+\xi,\qquad
 \epsilon'=\epsilon+a,\qquad
 \rho^{q\prime}=\rho^q+b,
\]
with nonnegative new radii.  If \(0<d^-\le|d^\star|\le d^+\),
\(|\xi|\le\zeta\), \(a\ge\underline a\), and
\(b\ge\underline b\), define
\[
 c=\zeta-\underline a,\qquad
 L^q=
 \begin{cases}
 d^+c-\underline b,&c\ge0,\\
 d^-c-\underline b,&c<0.
 \end{cases}
\]
Then every member of the new family has, along the same path,
\begin{equation}
 \mu'_{r,m}(\alpha)\ge
 \underline\mu_{r,m}-L^q_{r,m}.
 \label{eq:margin-transfer}
\end{equation}
If all transferred disk, coordinate, damping-facet, and annulus margins are
strictly positive, \(A\) remains a robust inner interval for every member of
the family.  If all are nonnegative, it remains a closed inner interval.
Otherwise this transfer is unknown and makes no outer-exclusion claim.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The triangle inequality gives
\[
 |q^\star-d^\star(\widetilde Z+\xi)|
 \le |q^\star-d^\star\widetilde Z|+|d^\star||\xi|.
\]
After adding the new radius,
\[
 \mu'\ge\mu-|d^\star|(\zeta-\underline a)+\underline b.
\]
Maximizing the affine expression
\(|d^\star|(\zeta-\underline a)-\underline b\) over
\([d^-,d^+]\) selects the displayed endpoint according to the sign of \(c\),
which proves \eqref{eq:margin-transfer}.  The old path already satisfies the
model equations and all unchanged priors.  Transferred nonnegative margins
therefore supply one witness for every \(\alpha\) and every admitted R2
perturbation; strict margins give the robust version.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Direction of the transfer theorem]
Increasing a response or independent morphology radius while holding its
centre fixed can only help inner feasibility, although it weakens exclusion
power and enlarges the outer parameter set.  Thus
Theorem~\ref{thm:r2-t1-transfer} is a budget for centre motion, radius
shrinkage, and prior tightening; it is not by itself a statement that a
larger data-error radius is higher quality.  Rebuilding the least-squares
selector from the new centres would prove a different path and is not a valid
replay of the old witness.  The current transfer certificate binds the
declared perturbation family and the old T1 certificate; binding that family
to a named R2 component-certificate bundle is a separate integration gate.
\end{remark}

For probabilistic components, the coverage event must be declared
simultaneously over every selected rate--harmonic disk used by R1.  Unless a
direct joint calibration is supplied, its failure budget includes all error
terms, windows, rates, and harmonics through a preregistered union bound.  An
empty R1 set obtained from such disks is therefore a confidence-qualified
rejection, not a deterministic contradiction.

\begin{corollary}[Certified minimum burn-in]
Let \(\epsilon_{\rm rest}\) contain every radius term except history and
dwell, and let \(\epsilon_\star>\epsilon_{\rm rest}\).  For the identity front
end, or after an independently calibrated filter-envelope factor is absorbed,
with
\[
 C_H(\alpha,\lambda)=|z_0|+C_{\rm pre}
 +\frac{U_{\rm pre}+2U_0}{\lambda},
\]
a sufficient dimensionless burn-in is
\[
 B_{\min}=\inf\left\{B\ge0:
 \frac{a_m}{|\widehat H_m|}
 \sup_{I\times\Lambda}C_H(\alpha,\lambda)
 E_\alpha(-\lambda B^\alpha)
 \le\epsilon_\star-\epsilon_{\rm rest}\right\}.
\]
The physical dwell to the right window endpoint must satisfy
\[
 T_{\rm dwell}\ge\tau_\star B_{\min}
 +\frac{2\pi N}{\widehat\omega_r}.
\]
This is a minimum for the declared certificate, not a physiological shortest
settling time.
The sum in \(C_H\) matches the separately budgeted history and dwell norms in
\eqref{eq:total-radius}: each is bounded by its coefficient times
\(E_\alpha(-\lambda B^\alpha)\).  A smaller
\(\max\{|z_0|+U_0/\lambda+C_{\rm pre},
(U_{\rm pre}+U_0)/\lambda\}\) is available only if their shared
\(M(s)-M(S+s)\) structure is certified as one joint residual budget rather
than added as two independent radii.
\end{corollary}

\begin{corollary}[Minimum deterministic energy SNR]
Suppose \(|Z_{r,m}|\ge\underline Z_{r,m}>0\), the target relative radius is
\(\eta\), and \(\eta\underline Z_{r,m}>\epsilon_{\rm rest}\).  Suppose in
addition that the declared estimator has a certified bridge
\(\bar r_{\rm noise}\le C_sN_2/\sqrt T\).  Exact continuous lock-in has
\(C_s=1\) and coefficient functional norm \(a_m=1\); discrete WLS requires a
separate sampling/reconstruction theorem before such a bridge may be used.
With
\[
 \operatorname{SNR}_{E,r,m}=
 \frac{|\widehat H_m|\underline Z_{r,m}\sqrt T}{N_2},
\]
the sufficient condition
\[
 \operatorname{SNR}_{E,r,m}\ge
 \frac{a_mC_s}{\eta-\epsilon_{\rm rest}/\underline Z_{r,m}}
\]
guarantees the target.  Under only pointwise worst-case noise, lengthening the
window need not improve this deterministic SNR.
\end{corollary}

\begin{remark}[R2 is conditionally closed, but not calibrated on real ECG]
\label{rem:r2-gap}
The algebraic data-to-disk theorem and the machine propagation of history,
dwell, sampling, optional quadrature, relative delay, and WLS Gram bounds are
now closed conditional on their declared primitive envelopes.  The project
does not yet possess independent real-ECG numerical bounds for R-time and
latent intra-beat phase error, measurement/model discrepancy, anti-aliasing or
interpolation, relative filter delay, Caputo prehistory, and the harmonic tail.
The component certificate also lists HRV, phase anchor, filter initialization,
measurement noise, and model residual as omitted terms, so it cannot by itself
issue a complete R2 pass.  Without every required bound,
\eqref{eq:response-disks} cannot be invoked on real ECG.  An absent bound
yields \texttt{NOT\_CERTIFIABLE}, not model rejection.
\end{remark}

\section{R3: worst-case ambiguity and design}

Let \(\mathfrak D\) be a compact design set, \(\Theta\) a compact nuisance
set, and \(f_{\mathcal D}(\alpha,\theta)\in\C^n\) a jointly continuous nominal
output map in \((\mathcal D,\alpha,\theta)\), with one fixed output dimension
\(n\) across designs.  Let
\[
 \mathcal U_{\mathcal D}=
 \{e:|e_j|\le\epsilon_j(\mathcal D)\},\qquad
 \epsilon_j(\mathcal D)\ge\epsilon_{\min}>0,
\]
with continuous radii.  Define
\[
 \Yset_{\mathcal D}(\alpha)=
 \{f_{\mathcal D}(\alpha,\theta)+e:
   \theta\in\Theta,e\in\mathcal U_{\mathcal D}\},
\quad
 \Aset_{\mathcal D}(y)=
 \{\alpha:y\in\Yset_{\mathcal D}(\alpha)\}.
\]
Write \(\mathcal O_{\mathcal D}=\bigcup_{\alpha\in I}
\Yset_{\mathcal D}(\alpha)\) for the reachable observation set, and define
the class of uniformly reliable set estimators by
\[
 \mathfrak R_{\mathcal D}=\left\{S:\mathcal O_{\mathcal D}\to2^I:
 \alpha\in S(f_{\mathcal D}(\alpha,\theta)+e)
 \quad\forall\alpha\in I,\ \theta\in\Theta,\ e\in\mathcal U_{\mathcal D}
 \right\}.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Minimal reliable set and ambiguity identity]
\label{thm:ambiguity}
Every deterministic set estimator guaranteed to contain the true order for
all admissible realizations must contain \(\Aset_{\mathcal D}(y)\) pointwise.
Moreover,
\begin{equation}
 \inf_{S\in\mathfrak R_{\mathcal D}}
 \sup_{y\in\mathcal O_{\mathcal D}}\diameter S(y)
 =\sup_{y\in\mathcal O_{\mathcal D}}
 \diameter\Aset_{\mathcal D}(y)=\Delta(\mathcal D),
 \label{eq:minimax-radius}
 \end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
 \quad
 \Delta(\mathcal D)=
 \max\{|\alpha-\beta|:
 \Yset_{\mathcal D}(\alpha)\cap
 \Yset_{\mathcal D}(\beta)\ne\varnothing\}.
 \label{eq:ambiguity}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Every \(\alpha\in\Aset_{\mathcal D}(y)\) can be the true order of an
admissible experiment producing \(y\), so a uniformly reliable set must
contain it.  Conversely, \(S(y)=\Aset_{\mathcal D}(y)\) is itself reliable,
which proves the first equality in \eqref{eq:minimax-radius}.  Two orders occur
in one consistency set exactly when their output sets intersect.  Compactness
and continuity attain the displayed maximum.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Separation modulus]
For \(0<\delta\le\diameter I\), let
\begin{align}
 s_{\mathcal D}(\alpha,\beta)&=
 \min_{\theta,\theta'\in\Theta}
 \max_j
 \frac{|f_{\mathcal D,j}(\alpha,\theta)
       -f_{\mathcal D,j}(\beta,\theta')|}
      {\epsilon_j(\mathcal D)},\\
 \mu_{\mathcal D}(\delta)&=
 \min_{\substack{\alpha,\beta\in I\\|\alpha-\beta|\ge\delta}}
 s_{\mathcal D}(\alpha,\beta).
 \label{eq:mu}
\end{align}
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Exact disk threshold and optimal-design existence]
\label{thm:design}
For product complex-disk errors,
\[
 \Yset_{\mathcal D}(\alpha)\cap\Yset_{\mathcal D}(\beta)\ne\varnothing
 \iff s_{\mathcal D}(\alpha,\beta)\le2,
\]
and hence
\[
 \Delta(\mathcal D)<\delta
 \iff \mu_{\mathcal D}(\delta)>2.
\]
For fixed \(\delta\), \(\mathcal D\mapsto\mu_{\mathcal D}(\delta)\) is
continuous and
\[
 \mathcal D_\delta^\star\in
 \arg\max_{\mathcal D\in\mathfrak D}\mu_{\mathcal D}(\delta)
\]
exists.  If the optimum exceeds \(2\), a maximizing design with the canonical
estimator \(S(y)=\Aset_{\mathcal D}(y)\) guarantees worst-case diameter less
than \(\delta\).  If the optimum is at most \(2\), no uniformly reliable
deterministic set estimator under any design in \(\mathfrak D\) can guarantee
diameter less than \(\delta\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
A common observation implies coordinatewise nominal-output differences at
most \(2\epsilon_j\).  Conversely, the midpoint of two nominal outputs whose
coordinate differences are at most \(2\epsilon_j\) lies in both disk products.
This proves the threshold.  Continuity follows by successive minimization of
a continuous function over fixed compact sets; maximization over compact
\(\mathfrak D\) attains an optimum.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Local close-rate condition numbers]
\label{thm:conditioning}
Fix \(x\), \(q\ne0\), \(\lambda>0\), and
\(\alpha\in\operatorname{int}I\).  Let \(\mathcal R_{2,h}\) denote the
locally defined physical two-rate recovery map at log-rates \(x,x+h\).  For
three equal log steps define
\[
 Q_h(W)=\frac{W(x+2h)-W(x)}{W(x+h)-W(x)},\qquad
 \mathcal R_{3,h}(W)=\frac1h\log(\operatorname{Re}Q_h(W)-1)
\]
on the open neighborhood where the denominator is nonzero,
\(\operatorname{Re}Q_h>1\), and the displayed value lies in
\(\operatorname{int}I\).  Thus \(\mathcal R_{3,h}\) is a declared real-part
projection diagnostic for off-model complex perturbations, not a physical
inverse of an arbitrary complex quotient.  For independent complex inverse-response
perturbations with the \(\ell_\infty\) norm,
\[
 \|D_W\mathcal R_{2,h}\|_{\infty\to\R}=\Theta(h^{-1}),\qquad
 \|D_W\mathcal R_{3,h}\|_{\infty\to\R}=\Theta(h^{-2})
\]
as \(h\to0^+\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For two rates, \(D_h=W(x+h)-W(x)=\Theta(h)\), so differentiating
\(C=W_1/(W_2-W_1)\) gives \(\Theta(h^{-2})\).  The derivative of \(H_C\)
with respect to \(C\) contains
\(e^{\beta h}-1=\Theta(h)\), while at the true order
\[
 \frac{\partial H_C}{\partial\beta}
 =\frac{e^{\alpha h}h}{e^{\alpha h}-1}
 +\frac{\lambda}{\nu_1^\alpha}
  \frac{\pi}{2\sin(\pi\alpha/2)}
 =\Theta(1).
\]
The implicit sensitivity is \(\Theta(h^{-1})\).  For three equal log steps,
\[
 F_h(\alpha)=1+e^{\alpha h},\qquad
 F_h'(\alpha)=he^{\alpha h}=\Theta(h),
\]
while \(D_WQ_h=\Theta(h^{-1})\).  The real-part projection has unit norm and
admits a perturbation direction attaining that order; composing it with
\(F_h^{-1}\) therefore gives \(\Theta(h^{-2})\).
\end{proof}

These are worst-direction condition numbers of the stated algebraic maps, not
information-theoretic lower bounds for all three-rate estimators.  The
corresponding finite-error expansions require respectively
\(\epsilon=o(h)\) and \(\epsilon=o(h^2)\); fixed nonzero error eventually
leaves the local regime.

\begin{proposition}[Finite frequencies do not identify mechanism]
\label{prop:rational-interpolant}
Let \(\{(s_j,z_j)\}_{j=1}^L\) be distinct finite frequency-response data with
\(s_j=i\omega_j\in i\R\), closed under complex conjugation with
conjugate-consistent targets.  For any
\(a>0\) and integer \(N\ge L\), there is a real-coefficient, stable, strictly
proper rational transfer function
\[
 H_{\rm rat}(s)=\frac{P(s)}{(s+a)^N}
\]
such that \(H_{\rm rat}(s_j)=z_j\) for every \(j\).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Interpolate the conjugate-consistent targets \(z_j(s_j+a)^N\) by the unique
polynomial \(P\) of degree at most \(L-1\).  Conjugate symmetry and uniqueness
make its coefficients real.  The denominator has only the stable pole
\(-a\), and \(N>\deg P\).
\end{proof}

Consequently, uniqueness inside the declared fractional family identifies an
effective surrogate order, not an intrinsic fractional physiological
mechanism.  Held-out transient prediction against integer-order rational
baselines is therefore essential.

\paragraph{Result semantics.}\mbox{}\par
R2 returns one of
\begin{center}
\texttt{PASS\_DETERMINISTIC}, \texttt{PASS\_PROBABILISTIC},\\
\texttt{NOT\_CERTIFIABLE}, \texttt{EXCLUDE\_PROTOCOL}.
\end{center}
Downstream \texttt{REJECT\_MODEL} requires R2 disks with declared simultaneous
coverage and an R1 proof that the entire preregistered joint domain is empty,
with no unknown leaf.  A probabilistic disk yields only a
confidence-qualified rejection.  Missing protocol bounds, singular
certificates, unresolved global boxes, and nonempty but overly wide order sets
remain \texttt{NOT\_CERTIFIABLE}.

\paragraph{Excluded claim.}
The theory outputs an order set.  It does not prove stability of an observer
implemented with a mismatched fractional derivative; that problem contains a
full-history operator mismatch and is deferred.

\paragraph{Prior-art boundary.}
Bounded complex-frequency set inversion for an isomorphic fractional
first-order model and complex-disk set estimation already exist
\cite{malti2010,khemane2012}, while frequency-domain fractional
identifiability and its practical sensitivity limits are established topics
\cite{nazarian2010}.  Fractional Kalman filters already estimate states,
parameters, and order, and recent high-order FO-EKF variants further reinforce
that \emph{FO-EKF} itself is not a novelty claim
\cite{sierociuk2006,yu2026}.  Robust fractional experiment design and generic
set-membership design also exist
\cite{abrashov2017,karimshoushtari2020}.  Finite-memory reset/truncation
observers are prior art \cite{rauhmalti2023,rauhlahme2025}; fractional ECG
generators and ECG-calibrated fractional McSharry models already exist
\cite{das2013,takha2024}; and contemporary cardiac twins expose serious
surface-ECG nonuniqueness \cite{gillette2021,grandits2025}.  Parameter-
dependent interval verification, affine sensitivity enclosures, existential
projection, and validated solution tubes are likewise established methods
\cite{neumaier1989,rump2010,goldsztejn2006,ponleitner2021}.  We therefore do
not propose a new interval Newton or validated-continuation method.  The defensible
contribution is restricted to their FO-ECG specialization: a dimensionless
two-rate quotient theorem modulo one common complex morphology/lead gauge, the
rank characterization in Lemma~\ref{lem:selector-rank}, the analytic witness
tube paired with the outer disk cover, the frozen-witness finite-record margin
transfer, the joint multi-rate consistency semantics, and the finite-record
uncertainty/design chain.  We do not claim priority with the word first.
